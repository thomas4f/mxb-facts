For authors

Every tool gets the same questions, answered with evidence from a published prompt. Below is what earns a Good answer, and a check you can run on your tool before a release.

No author sees a card before it is published, the maintainer's own tool included; then you get a short note with its link if you have a public contact, and anyone can correct it on GitHub. A licence that forbids analysis gets a card of public facts only, and no score.

What earns a Good answer

The answer each question needs for a Good mark. A Note or a Concern can be the honest answer for what a tool is for: a mark says what a player should know, and what to change, if anything, is up to you. The cost is what a Concern takes off its area, at most (how scores are worked out).

Check your tool before you release

{% set ck = SITE.authors_check %}
  1. Add the check to your repository: save it as {{ ck.path }} (download it). Claude Code picks it up as a skill; for other agents, add this line to AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md:{{ copy_block('agents-line', ck.agents_line) }}
  2. Ask your agent to run it before a release. It follows our prompt, steps 1 to 5, and lists every answer that wouldn't be Good, with its evidence. It changes nothing on its own. Your copy uses the rules as they were when you downloaded it ({{ SITE.commit[:12] }}), so a result can be repeated; when the rules have changed since, it tells you and asks which to use.

Prefer the full run? Clone the repository, open Claude Code in it, paste the analysis prompt and stop after step 5, as the check does.

Once your tool has a card

Everything below is at the foot of your tool's page, under "For the tool's author".

  1. Go through it with your agent: download the card and paste one prompt. Your agent follows each answer's evidence into your code; we don't suggest fixes.
  2. Release, then request a re-check. The new release gets a new card; the old one stays.
  3. Something wrong, missing, or context to add? Use "Comment on …" under any answer's evidence, Good ones included, or the comment form; it is answered in public.
  4. Show the card on your pages: the card image for a mods-site post, a forum or your site (it always shows your newest card), the page's address for Discord, which draws the same image, or a badge for a README, pinned to one release. Each comes ready to copy as HTML, Markdown or BBCode.
